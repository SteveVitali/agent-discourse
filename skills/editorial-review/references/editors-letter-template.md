# Editor's Letter Template (Phase 6)

The letter is the anchor deliverable: readable start-to-finish by the author
without opening the other dossier files, referencing them for depth. It speaks
to the author as a respected professional — direct, specific, exacting, on the
author's side. No compliment sandwiches; no hedging that blunts a finding; no
padding for symmetry.

```markdown
# Editorial Review: <piece title>

- **Draft:** <path/URL, date reviewed>
- **Standard applied:** <target venue/audience, and whether given or inferred>
- **Draft stage assumed:** <early / near-final — and how that scoped the review>
- **Dossier:** claim ledger (`01-…`), discourse report (`02-…`),
  second read (`05-…`)

## Verdict

<Accept / Minor revisions / Major revisions / Reject and rework /
Reject premise> — one paragraph of justification, stating plainly what kind of
piece this is, how close it is to its own ambition, and what class of work
remains.

**The one thing:** the single change that would most improve the piece, in two
or three sentences. If the author does nothing else, they do this.

## The piece's project

Two or three paragraphs: thesis in its strongest form, the argument's actual
skeleton, who it is for, and what success would be. This is the steelman — the
author should read it and say "yes, that is the piece I was writing." (If the
independent second read understood a different piece, say so here: the draft
is not transmitting its own thesis.)

## Strengths

What is genuinely working, each anchored to a passage — the arguments that
land, the sections that sing, the places the piece is smarter than its likely
critics. These are protection orders for revision: name what must survive.

## Major issues

3–7, ranked by severity. Each:

### 1. <One-line claim of the defect>
- **Where:** quoted passage(s) with location.
- **The problem:** the analysis — why this fails, with the steelman
  considered and, where relevant, the ledger entry or discourse finding.
- **Why it matters here:** the consequence at the target venue / with the
  intended audience.
- **Direction:** the concrete fix — which may be "cut it." Options are fine;
  vagueness is not.

## Minor issues

Compact list, each anchored: local unclarities, fixable overstatements,
missing citations, structural nits, ledger items of the
"substantially accurate — adjust the number" kind.

## Fact-check summary

The ledger's summary block, plus prose on the pattern: is the factual
substrate sound? Every Contradicted / Misleading / Unsupported finding named
with its fix. Point to `01-claim-ledger.md` for the full trail. Include what
checked out *better* than expected — report against interest.

## The discursive situation

From `02-…`: the conversation the piece is entering; nearest predecessors and
what this adds beyond them (the originality verdict, stated plainly); the
strongest existing counterargument and how the piece currently fares against
it; positions the piece mischaracterizes; work it should cite.

## Reception forecast

How this lands on publication day with the actual intended audience: who
engages, what gets quoted, what it will be pattern-matched to, the strongest
hostile-but-fair reading, and the most likely misreadings — with what the text
could do to preempt each.

## Line notes

Prose patterns with exemplars (habit → 2–3 quoted instances → one rewritten
example). Selective, not exhaustive; scale to draft stage.

## Scorecard

Per `references/rubric.md`: the table, the fuller justification of the lowest
dimension, and the one-point-improvement notes.

## Revision roadmap

The ordered plan for the next draft:

1. **Structural surgery first** — cuts, moves, merges (from Major issues /
   Phase 4). Never polish prose that may not survive.
2. **Argument repairs** — the reasoning fixes, in dependency order.
3. **Research follow-ups** — what to read or verify before redrafting, with
   the starting sources found in Phases 1–2 (URLs).
4. **Craft passes** — the prose-pattern fixes, once the structure is stable.
5. **Open questions for the author** — genuine forks (audience, scope,
   framing) the editor cannot decide; what each choice would entail.

## Considered and rejected

Findings from the second read (or your own deliberation) evaluated and not
adopted, each with the reason — the author sees the judgment, not just its
output.
```

Cut a section only when it is genuinely empty (e.g., no line notes requested
on an early draft) — and say so rather than deleting silently.
