# Scoring Rubric (Phase 6)

Eight dimensions, each scored 1–5 against the anchors below. The rubric
summarizes the review; it never substitutes for it. Scores unsupported by
specific findings in the dossier are invalid.

## The scale

The anchors are absolute, pegged to the piece's target venue — not relative to
typical online writing.

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| **5** | Exemplary. Among the best work in this genre at this venue; you must name a published comparison to award it. Rare. |
| **4** | Publishable at the target venue after minor revision. No major issue touches this dimension. |
| **3** | Competent but below the bar; a serious venue returns it for major revision on this dimension. Typical for a good draft. |
| **2** | Fundamental rework needed; the dimension has a structural failure, not a polish problem. |
| **1** | Fatally flawed on this dimension as conceived. |

## Calibration rules

- **Honesty beats kindness.** A rubric where everything lands on 3–4 is a
  failed rubric. Good drafts by smart authors typically earn a mix of 2s, 3s,
  and 4s; 5s must be argued for against a named exemplar.
- **Consistency with findings is mandatory.** The scores must be entailed by
  the dossier: a Contradicted or Misleading Tier 1 claim caps *Evidence &
  accuracy* at 2; an ignored strongest-counterargument caps *Argument rigor*
  at 3; an unwitting reinvention of an existing position caps *Originality &
  situatedness* at 2; a thesis the second read stated materially differently
  caps *Thesis & contribution* at 3.
- **Justify every score in one or two sentences that cite findings** (by
  major-issue number or ledger entry). The lowest-scoring dimension gets a
  fuller justification — it is usually "the one thing."

## Dimensions

1. **Thesis & contribution** — Is there one clear, contestable central claim,
   and does it matter? Would the target reader, granting the whole argument,
   have learned something significant?
2. **Argument rigor** — Acceptability, relevance, and sufficiency of the
   support; scope-matching; hidden premises; quality of inferences; whether
   the strongest objections are engaged rather than the convenient ones.
3. **Evidence & accuracy** — The ledger, in one number: reliability of the
   piece's factual substrate and the honesty of its use of sources.
4. **Originality & situatedness** — The Phase 2 verdict: novelty of claim or
   synthesis, awareness of predecessors, fairness to opposing positions, and
   whether the piece knows which conversation it is in.
5. **Structure** — Architecture, sequencing, proportion, opening and ending;
   whether every section earns its place.
6. **Prose** — Clarity, precision, economy, and control at the sentence
   level; freedom from cliché, jargon, and boilerplate cadence.
7. **Voice & audience fit** — A distinct, consistent voice, in the right
   register for the intended reader; authority earned rather than asserted.
8. **Intellectual honesty** — Claims scoped to their support; uncertainty and
   limitations acknowledged; opposing views represented as their holders
   would recognize; no motte-and-bailey retreat under pressure.

## Scorecard format

```markdown
| Dimension | Score | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Thesis & contribution | 3 | Thesis migrates between §1 and §4 (Major issue 2). |
| ... | ... | ... |
```

Follow the table with the fuller justification of the lowest dimension, and
one sentence on what a one-point improvement on each of the two weakest
dimensions would require — this feeds the revision roadmap.

No aggregate score. An average launders a 1 in argument rigor through a 5 in
prose; the verdict in the editor's letter is the only summary judgment.
